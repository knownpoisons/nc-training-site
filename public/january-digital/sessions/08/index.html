03  The system

Three moves, on repeat.

The whole business model in three moves. Clone the shape, swap the contents. The second run is always faster because the system already exists.

1
Media Data
Pull what's actually performing from the channels you manage.
2
Creative
Make the work, shaped by the data, at machine speed.
3
Market
Ship it, read the numbers, feed them back into move one.
Clone, don't rebuild
Duplicate the Carhartt Flora workflow for each new client. The shape is built. Swap the contents and run.
No silos
Anyone can open a colleague's Flora workflow and continue. Skills are non-siloed now.
Strip and share
When a Claude workflow works, remove the client info and export a reusable prompt. The team levels up together.
The second run is faster
Everything hard about Carhartt is done. The next client inherits the chain, the prompts, the feedback loop.
"I am not the creative genius that sometimes I claim to be. I just added a wrench. What's my wrench for Supergoop? What would Jeremy do? Do that and you start to flex those muscles."— Jeremy Somers
04  Claude workflows

Fill gaps, don't replace software.

The single best prompt you can use: "What information or context would make this better?" Let the model tell you what it needs. Drill through four context layers — agency, client, campaign, SKU.

Agency level
Who you are, what you do, how you work. The identity context that shapes every output.
Client level
Brand, category, audience, tone. The guardrails that keep creative on-brand.
Campaign level
Objective, channels, formats, timing. The brief that shapes the brief.
SKU level
The specific product, its features, its flat lay. The detail that makes the output real.
"It's not about what am I making. It's problem solving. I had a gap between the information I had and what actually needed to be done. So I built a tool that filled the gap. And this whole thing took me 15 minutes."— Jeremy Somers
05  The 30-day plan

One client. One job. Ship in week one.

Each person picks one client account to apply the process to first. The training gave you the tools. The 30-day plan gives you the deadline.

Which client
The account where you know the creative could be better and you already have the data.
First job
The campaign, the brief, the target. Be specific — specificity beats ambiguity.
Proof of concept
Use the Carhartt data when pitching internally or to clients. The numbers are the pitch.
Momentum
The system and templates exist to prevent stalling. The risk isn't capability; it's losing the thread.
"Specificity beats ambiguity. You don't say 'I think I might do Supergoop, I'll just wait until something comes in.' No — you go out and you get that sunscreen project and you tell them what they're going to do."— Jeremy Somers
"You guys will lose momentum quickly. And what ends up happening is then Luisa has to do pretty much everything. And collectively we love her too much to let her do everything and do our jobs too."— Jeremy Somers
