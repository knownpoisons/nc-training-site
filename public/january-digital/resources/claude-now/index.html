The Expert Panel

Before your work goes to a real creative director, a real client, or a real customer — run it past five AI experts who have no bad days, no politics, and no shortage of time.

01
Performance Marketer
Does it stop the scroll? Will it convert? Judges the work purely on attention and action.
02
Creative Director
Is the idea original? Is it on brand? Would it win the room?
03
Brand Guardian
What will the client never sign off on? Catches the things you stop seeing after hour three.
04
Skeptical Client
The hardest internal critic. If the panel can survive this one, the real client call is easy.
05
The Customer
Doesn't owe you anything. Doesn't care about your process. Would they stop, look, and act?
Master prompt — The Panel

Spin up a panel of five experts for this work: a performance marketer who only cares about stopping the scroll, a creative director who only cares about originality and brand, a brand guardian who knows what the client will never sign off on, a skeptical client, and the end customer who owes us nothing. Run [this folder / this work / this campaign] past all five. Come back with a synthesized report — actionable to-do’s, not 19 pages. Tell me where each expert agrees and where they disagree.

“You will feel so much more confident and get much better at setting feedback if you run this process and then do your own round of revisions before you send it to them.”— Jeremy, Session 09

The Scorer

Layer this on top of the Panel. Don’t just get opinions — get numbers, rankings, and the exact change prompts to fix what’s weak.

  • Score against the top 20 metrics from the brief
  • Average scores, rank every element highest to lowest
  • Explain why each score landed where it did
  • Generate the change prompts so you can paste them straight into Flora
Master prompt — The Scorer

Take [this folder / this work] and the brief I gave you. Score and rank every element against the top 20 metrics this work should be measured on — all referenced from the brief. Average the scores. Tell me which got the highest and why, which got the lowest and why, and then give me every change I need to make to get each element as close to 100 out of 100 as possible. Then write me the Flora change prompts so I can act on them immediately.

“The quality of your work is defined by the quality of the questions that you ask your Claudette.”— Jeremy, Session 09

Connectors

Claude is already connected to your Slack, Drive, Dropbox, and email. Now tell it what to do when things land there.

  • Client feedback arrives — Claude synthesizes it, compares against the brief, and tells you what to do
  • A Granola transcript drops — Claude extracts the action items before you’ve finished your coffee
  • A brief changes in Drive — Claude flags what moved and what it means for your current work
Master prompt — Connectors

You’re connected to my Slack, Drive, and email. Every time I receive client feedback on [this project], go read it, compare it against the brief I gave you, synthesize what changed, tell me where we went right and wrong, and give me a prioritized list of what to do next. Don’t wait for me to ask — just do it.

Skills

A skill is a reusable instruction set that Claude follows every time you trigger it. Build them once, use them forever.

What to build first

  • Branded documents — PPTs, reports, spreadsheets that always come out JD-branded, correctly formatted, in seconds
  • Brief intake — take a raw client brief and produce a structured creative brief with all the fields your team needs
  • Campaign QA — run the Panel + Scorer as a single skill before anything ships to client
  • Post-call processing — Granola transcript in, action items + follow-up draft out

Memory

Claude remembers what you tell it to. Brand guidelines, client preferences, your workflow quirks, the feedback pattern from a specific stakeholder. It builds up over time and makes every conversation smarter than the last.

  • Tell it your brand voice, client’s no-go zones, and approval patterns
  • It carries this across every conversation — no re-briefing
  • Update or correct it any time: “forget that, the client changed their mind”

The rule

“Don’t ever accept the first thing that she brings back to you. Assume that you didn’t ask the right amount of information.”— Jeremy, Session 09

The quality of your Claude output — and thereby your entire work that comes out of you — is dictated by the questions you ask. Push it to think laterally. Give it references. Ask it to suggest ideas and then let your brain riff on them. Never settle for the first answer.
